{\large\bfseries Response to the reviews of AESCTE-D-26-06017}\\[2pt]
Revised and retitled: ``Equal-Budget Benchmark of Surrogate-Assisted Search Strategies for Conceptual Design of a Tailless Blended-Wing-Body UAV with an Euler-Calibrated Stability Constraint''
\end{center}

I thank both reviewers. Their central criticisms were correct: the original submission attributed a gain to the surrogate without an equal-budget control, described a geometric constraint whose activity was never measured, and validated nothing beyond the lattice. The manuscript has been rebuilt around the experiments they asked for, and the conclusions have changed as a result. Three points should be stated at the outset.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
    \item The active-learning method of the original submission is not the best strategy. TuRBO is ($\NvMeanTurbo \pm \NvSdTurbo$\,km against $\NvMeanAL \pm \NvSdAL$\,km), and the original loop is not better than an adaptive local search without a surrogate ($\NvMeanAdapt \pm \NvSdAdapt$\,km). The paper now reports this as its main result.
    \item While preparing this revision I audited every statement of the paper against the code. The audit found two errors in my own baselines and one incorrect mathematical claim, described in Section~\ref{sec:corrections} of this letter. All are corrected and disclosed in the manuscript.
    \item All numbers in the manuscript are generated by scripts from the run files; the runs made before each correction are retained and reported in Table~\ref{tab:asrun}.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Reviewer 1}

\cmt{Provide a controlled comparison of the geometric manifold against an unconstrained parameterization.}
\rsp{Done, with a result that does not support the original claim. In a ten-seed paired experiment with the blend-chord bound widened so that inversion is possible, the search with the chord clamp reached \NclampMean\,km and the search without it \NnoclampMean\,km (Welch $p = \NclampP$; Section~\ref{sec:loft}, Table~\ref{tab:loft}). Inside the bounds of the original campaign the clamp was never active. The manuscript therefore describes the clamp as a validity guard and makes no performance claim for it. In addition, the statement that the loft is monotone by construction has been withdrawn: the implemented loft has a cubic leading edge and a straight trailing edge, its center-body chord is monotone only under the condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mono}, and \NvMonoViolShare\% of the feasible designs violate that condition by a median of \NvMonoMedian\,mm (maximum \NvMonoMax\,mm). Section~\ref{sec:loft_def} gives the correct definition and these numbers, and the planform figure has been redrawn from the geometry code. The 1,000-sample inversion study is retained with its area-matched control, which shows no efficiency difference at equal reference area.}

\cmt{Compare active learning with random sampling and Latin hypercube sampling at equal budget; the +7.5\,\% gain is not attributed.}
\rsp{Section~\ref{sec:budget_results} now compares eight strategies on the same 9,800-design pool with 287 further evaluations each and 10 to 20 seeds (Tables~\ref{tab:budget} and~\ref{tab:tests}, Fig.~\ref{fig:equal_budget}). The ordering of the leading strategies is the same when the budget is cut at 50 to 200 evaluations (Table~\ref{tab:anytime}) and when the pool is reduced to 288, 1{,}000 or 3{,}000 designs (Section~\ref{sec:pool_size}, Table~\ref{tab:pool}); with ten independently generated 1{,}000-design pools the lead of TuRBO over the adaptive local search keeps its sign ($\NpIndTvAD$\,km) but is not significant at the 5\% level ($p = \NpIndTvAP$), which the manuscript states.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
    \item Latin hypercube and uniform sampling do not improve on the pool (one of ten uniform runs exceeds it marginally).
    \item Static local search reaches $\NvMeanLocTen \pm \NvSdLocTen$\,km; letting its parent set follow new discoveries adds \NvDAdaptvLocTen\,km. This adaptive local search has the same batch structure and parent set as the active-learning loop and no surrogate.
    \item The active-learning loop as originally implemented reaches $\NvMeanAL \pm \NvSdAL$\,km, which is $\NvDsALvAdapt$\,km relative to the adaptive local search (Welch $p \NvPALvAdapt$, bootstrap interval $\NvCIALvAdapt$\,km). The gain claimed in the original submission is therefore not attributable to the surrogate.
    \item The loop was the only strategy that could propose designs violating the washout ordering, and spent \NvWashAL\% of its budget on them. With a repair that sorts the twist variables (AL-R) it reaches $\NvMeanALR \pm \NvSdALR$\,km, \NvDALRvAdapt\,km above the adaptive local search (Holm $p \NvHALRvAdapt$).
    \item TuRBO reaches $\NvMeanTurbo \pm \NvSdTurbo$\,km and leads AL-R by \NvDTurbovALR\,km (Welch $p \NvPTurbovALR$, Holm $p \NvHTurbovALR$, interval $\NvCITurbovALR$\,km). I do not claim that AL-R matches TuRBO.
\end{enumerate}
Adaptive local search alone recovers \NvShareAdapt\% of the gain of the best strategy over the pool. All tests are two-sided; the headline family is Holm-corrected; runs that stopped early are included.}

\cmt{Check rank consistency between the low-fidelity and a higher-fidelity aerodynamic model.}
\rsp{Section~\ref{sec:validation} of the main text and Sections~\ref{sec:grid_convergence} to~\ref{sec:cfd_validation} of the Supplementary Material report four checks. (i) Panel refinement from 672 to 3,584 panels on four designs (Table~\ref{tab:grid}). (ii) AeroBuildup on \NxN\ designs: Spearman $\rho = \NxRho$ overall and $\NxRhoTop$ among the better half, with a median $L/D$ ratio of \NxRatioMed, so ranking agrees but absolute drag does not. (iii) NeuralFoil section drag against the strip estimate (Table~\ref{tab:visc}). (iv) SU2 Euler solutions on five designs (Table~\ref{tab:cfd}), later extended to \NcalN\ designs to calibrate the static margin (Section~\ref{sec:sm_calibration}): $SM_{\mathrm{Euler}} = \NcalA + \NcalB\,SM_{\mathrm{VLM}}$, $R^2 = \NcalRsq$. The fit was then frozen and tested on \NoosN\ further run champions (Table~\ref{tab:oos}): root-mean-square error \NoosRmse, largest error \NoosMax. The surrogate's own rank correlation in the top decile of the pool is weak, which Section~\ref{sec:distribution_shift} now reports. The limitations state plainly that no viscous three-dimensional solution and no experiment are available and that ranges are comparative.}

\cmt{Analyse false negatives of the feasibility classifier (55\,\% recall).}
\rsp{Section~\ref{sec:classifier_audit} audits the classifier on five stratified splits: recall is \NfnRecall\% overall and \NfnTenRecall\% among the top 10\% of designs by range, and \NfnWithin\% of the false negatives lie within 10\% of a constraint boundary. Beyond the audit, Section~\ref{sec:feas_ablation} now tests what the classifier does to the search (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}). Without the classifier weight the loop finds no feasible design in any of 20 runs; a hard gate reaches the same range as the sigmoid weight at the full budget but much later; and the same weight applied to TuRBO raises its feasible fraction but not its mean range ($\NvDsTurboFeasFixvTurbo$\,km, $p \NvPTurboFeasFixvTurbo$). The classifier is thus necessary for this loop, and its imperfect recall does not prevent the loop from reaching the high-range region.}

\cmt{Remove claims of global optimality and strong wording.}
\rsp{Removed throughout. Section~\ref{sec:bound_prices} shows why such a claim would be wrong: the best designs sit at the battery-mass limit, and raising that limit by $0.2$\,kg gains \NbpBmassGain\,km, more than the difference between the leading strategies. The text has also been rewritten in plain language.}

\cmt{Add a summary table separating the three contributions.}
\rsp{Table~\ref{tab:claims} lists each claim with its evidence and its scope limit.}

\cmt{Shorten Sections~3 and~4.}
\rsp{Both were rewritten. They now state only what the code does, with the equations it implements.}

\cmt{Specify seeds, initial samples, budgets, termination criteria and RANS details.}
\rsp{Section~\ref{sec:protocol} gives the pool, the seeds, the surrogate architecture (five networks of two hidden layers of 64 units), the classifier, the acquisition and its optimizer, the batch of six candidates per cycle, and the exhaustion rule. The CFD is Euler, not RANS, and is described as such (Section~\ref{sec:cfd_validation}).}

\section*{Reviewer 2}

\cmt{Describe the panel grid and validate the 3D effects with CFD.}
\rsp{Section~\ref{sec:vlm_discretization} and Fig.~\ref{fig:vlm_discretization} describe the lattice: 29 sections per side, two spanwise by twelve chordwise panels per interval, 1,344 panels in total. Three-dimensional effects are checked with SU2 Euler solutions on meshes of about $1.2 \times 10^5$ nodes, first on five designs and then on \NcalN. Euler lift agrees with the lattice within a median ratio of \NcalCLmed. The Euler static margin is higher than the lattice margin by a nearly constant \NcalA, so the lattice is not conservative for the upper limit: the cap of $0.25$ corresponds to a lattice value of \NcalCapEquiv, and the best design of the original campaign has an Euler margin of $0.258$. Table~\ref{tab:sm_rerank} re-ranks every run under the calibrated constraint; the ranking of the strategies does not change, but the best design of a minority of runs of most strategies lies beyond the calibrated limit.}

\section{Corrections found by the author's own audit}
\label{sec:corrections}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Energy basis of two baselines.} The first implementations of TuRBO and of the adaptive local search compared new evaluations and pool designs on two different energy bases, a factor of $1.30$ in favor of new designs. Both were rerun with a consistent basis. For TuRBO the mean changed by $\NvPairedTurboD$\,km (paired $p \NvPairedTurboP$); for the adaptive local search it rose by \NvDAdaptvAdaptOld\,km (Welch $p \NvPAdaptvAdaptOld$), which makes the surrogate-free control stronger. Table~\ref{tab:asrun} shows both versions. The bound-price runs and the TuRBO runs with the classifier weight were also repeated with the corrected implementation, and the repeated runs are the ones reported.
  \item \textbf{Asymmetric constraint handling.} Only the active-learning loop could propose designs that violate the washout ordering. The AL-R arm removes the asymmetry, and both arms are reported.
  \item \textbf{Monotonicity of the loft.} The proposition and proof in the earlier draft described a Hermite chord law that the code does not implement. They are withdrawn (see the first response).
  \item \textbf{Battery placement.} The original text described a solver that positions the battery for every design. The evaluator fixes the battery centroid at $0.82\,c_r$ and enforces static margin and trim as constraints; the text now says so, and Section~\ref{sec:robustness} tests the sensitivity to that choice.
  \item \textbf{Battery energy density.} The database was computed at $160\,\mathrm{Wh/kg}$ while the text cited another value. All ranges are now reported on the basis of a specified 48-cell pack, $\NeU\,\mathrm{Wh/kg}$ usable; range is linear in this quantity, so comparisons are unaffected.
  \item \textbf{Model descriptions.} The surrogate architecture, the number of candidates per cycle, the acquisition function, the profile-drag formula, the trim-drag term, the angle-of-attack solution, the widened bounds, the washout constraint $g_8$ and the additional geometric rejections are now stated as implemented.
  \item \textbf{References.} All entries were checked against publisher records; entries that could not be verified were removed.
\end{itemize}
